The Field

How Ultraviolet compares to everything else putting assets on Bitcoin.

Ultraviolet is a research project. It has a working core, an end-to-end demo in CI, and real payments live on signet. It is formally modelled, unaudited, and nobody outside the project has reviewed its proof circuits. The incumbents below ship to production. Read every row with that asymmetry in mind.

The strongest incumbent

Taproot Assets

Lightning Labs' protocol is the most shipped competitor. It is at v0.8.0 as of July 2026, with USDT live on Lightning since March 2026, static reusable addresses, multi-path payments, and RFQ edge liquidity. It is also a cousin. Both protocols validate on the client, keep asset data off-chain, and use Bitcoin as the anchor. Three architectural commitments separate them:

OWNERSHIP

The UTXO is the asset

A Taproot Asset lives inside a taproot output, so ownership is a secp256k1 Schnorr key. That is elegant: the asset commitment hides inside an ordinary-looking spend. The costs are a UTXO per holder, an on-chain spend per transfer, and total quantum exposure with no PQ roadmap. Ultraviolet instead puts hash-based keys in client-side data, publishes keyless 64-byte records, and lets you receive with an empty wallet. A quantum thief who steals the anchor UTXO steals nothing.

VALIDATION

Proofs grow forever

Provenance proofs grow O(lineage), meaning they grow with every past owner back to issuance. They fatten at each hop and reveal history and amounts to every recipient. No zero-knowledge layer exists or is planned. Ultraviolet's receiver walks the lineage too, and that walk is what stops inflation. But each hop is one fixed-size 208 KB zero-knowledge STARK (a proof built from hashes, not curves), checked in 1.5 ms, measured. Amounts stay confidential along the whole lineage. Collapsing the walk itself is the accumulator, which is future work.

SPEED

Channels, shipped

TA's decisive present-tense advantage: multi-asset Lightning channels in production. If you need trustless instant settlement this quarter, TA wins. You pay for it in the other two columns. Ultraviolet's answer today is payments visible in Bitcoin's mempool within seconds. Its kernel-native eltoo channels (the latest signed state replaces the previous one) and its hash-locked gateway swaps into Lightning are designed and formally modelled, not built.

Real-world assets sharpen the quantum argument. Tokenized treasuries and bonds are long-duration instruments. A 10-year note issued on classical rails in 2026 must remain unforgeable into the mid-2030s. That is the exact window CRQC estimates fight over: forecasts of when a cryptographically relevant quantum computer arrives. Payments can migrate when the threat lands. Issued long-dated assets have to outlive it.

The verdict

Adjacent designs

SuperScalar & channel factories

SuperScalar is the strongest classical answer to receiving without a UTXO. It puts 127 clients behind one MuSig2 taproot output, with no covenants and no trust in the Lightning service provider (LSP). It was implemented and exercised on signet (July 2026), with a real on-chain penalty proving the "revealed-secret poison." Two contrasts follow. First, the factory's entire trust model rests on a single aggregated Schnorr point holding 128 parties' funds. Factories concentrate quantum exposure where Ultraviolet's keyless records eliminate it. Second, the report names its own safety-critical core: "the double-sign defense is where the whole safety rests." Each signer has to persist a refusal to co-sign conflicting states. That is the equivocation problem, and Ultraviolet's base rail solves it structurally. Each spend derives one deterministic nullifier, a spend marker fixed by the coin rather than chosen by the spender. The first on-chain occurrence wins, and there is no co-signing state at all. The wallet does keep one persisted log, the record of what each one-time key signed. Losing that log costs an in-flight payment, never safety. The cheater-funds-the-penalty pattern is prior art for the shelved optional speed layer's bonds-are-notes design.

Also in the field. Shielded CSV (Blockstream/Alpen/ZeroSync) contributed the architecture Ultraviolet builds on: 64-byte nullifiers and client-side proofs. Its constant-size receive is its own achievement, and Ultraviolet trades that away for per-hop hiding proofs until the accumulator lands. But its nullifier check is a Schnorr verification, so it is not post-quantum. RGB v0.12 modernized its VM but removed confidential amounts, never shipped history compression, and remains UTXO-bound.
